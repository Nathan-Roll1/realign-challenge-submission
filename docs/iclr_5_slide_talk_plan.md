# Five-slide ICLR talk plan

Title: **Dogs aren't Cars: The Dangers of Poor Objectives for Representational Alignment**

This should be a five-slide talk, not a compressed paper. The audience should leave with one sentence:

> CKA is a useful measurement, but it measures agreement on a stimulus set; if the set is broad, category agreement can drown out stimulus-level representation.

## Presentation constraints

The plan follows three practical rules from conference presentation guidance:

- ICLR explicitly recommends colorblind-friendly documents and avoiding color-only contrasts in slides and posters: https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2020/PresentationGuide
- NeurIPS spotlight guidance is relevant for short research talks: keep the talk simple, visual, accessible to non-specialists, and avoid trying to present every technical detail: https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2014/AuthorsAndPresenters
- Clear-talk guidance from academic presentation teaching is the strongest fit here: one point per slide, put the point in the title, explain every figure, and remake paper figures for the talk instead of dropping in dense paper plots.

## Five-slide script

1. "CKA does not compare two networks in the abstract. It compares the geometry they induce on a particular set of images. That sounds like a detail, but in this challenge it became the whole problem."

2. "When the images span many categories, dogs, cars, birds, buildings, the similarity matrix has a category skeleton. Centering changes the matrix, but it does not make stimulus composition disappear. Most competent classifiers agree on the skeleton, so high CKA can mean shared task competence."

3. "The Blue track asked us to maximize CKA by choosing models. We treated it as a densest-20-subgraph problem over the registry. The submitted set is what the objective selected. Before calling this universality, we should ask what shared signal those models had the least freedom to avoid."

4. "The Red track let us choose images instead. We chose dog and canid images: enough variation to search, but much less category contrast. Now models cannot get high agreement just by separating dogs from cars. They have to agree on pose, texture, background, breed, crop, and scene. That is where architecture families disagree."

5. "So the warning is simple: CKA can be useful, but the score is conditional on the stimulus set. If a paper reports one alignment number on one broad dataset, it may be reporting category agreement. At minimum, report broad and restricted stimulus conditions separately."

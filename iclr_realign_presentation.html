Dogs aren’t Cars:
The Dangers of Poor Objectives for Representational Alignment

CKA is treated as a model similarity measure. In practice, it can reward agreement on the task before it rewards agreement on representations.

The title is the argument: CKA can make shared task competence look like shared representation.
Dogs aren’t Cars1 / 12
Central claim

CKA conflates two kinds of agreement.

One is agreement about categories. The other is agreement about individual stimuli inside a category. The first is often much louder.

Category agreement

Dogs are closer to dogs than to cars. Most competent classifiers agree on this.

Stimulus agreement

Which dog images are similar to which other dog images? Here model families diverge.

This slide should be slow. The whole talk is category agreement versus stimulus agreement.
The objective problem2 / 12
What CKA computes

It compares image–image geometry, not features directly.

Each model maps the same image set into activations. CKA compares the centered Gram matrices of those activations.

H = I − 1n 11T Kc = HXXTH Lc = HYYTH CKA(X,Y) = ⟨Kc, Lc⟩F∥Kc∥F ∥Lc∥F A cosine between two centered similarity matrices.
No derivation. CKA asks whether two models induce the same similarity matrix over the same stimuli.
Centered Gram geometry3 / 12
Why the objective is dangerous

When the set spans many categories, category structure dominates.

The centered matrix still contains the block structure of the task. Shared competence can masquerade as shared representation.

X = A + R A: category-level structure R: stimulus-level residual Broad sets make A the easiest way to agree.
Centering does not magically remove category structure. It changes the object but leaves stimulus composition central.

High CKA can mean “not enough capacity to disagree.”

If an objective rewards agreement on the dominant category signal, it can mistake limited representational freedom for deep alignment.

Red track7 / 12
Dogs aren’t cars

Remove the easy contrast and the residual becomes visible.

Within dogs, texture, pose, silhouette, scene, crop, and background matter. CNNs, ViTs, CLIP-style models, and masked autoencoders need not organize those axes the same way.

Connect this to texture-vs-shape work and work on what ViTs and CLIP attend to relative to CNNs.
Within-category disagreement8 / 12
Both tracks point at the same flaw

The CKA objective rewards the wrong thing unless we control the stimulus distribution.

Blue side

Maximizing CKA can select models that share the dominant task signal, not models with universal internal structure.

Red side

Restricting to a fine-grained superclass suppresses category agreement and exposes stimulus-level disagreement.

This is the synthesis slide: one objective, two failure modes.

What to do instead

Report alignment as a conditional measurement.

Control categories

Separate broad-category agreement from within-category organization.

Vary stimuli

Run CKA on broad sets and on fine-grained restricted sets.

Interrogate objectives

Ask what the score rewards before calling it representational alignment.

This turns the red-team result into a constructive recommendation.
Reporting alignment11 / 12
Takeaway

CKA can answer a useful question. But the question is not “do these models learn the same representation?” It is “do these models induce the same geometry on this stimulus set?”

Dogs aren’t cars. If the objective forgets that, alignment scores can become scores for shared task competence.

Likely questions

Is this metric hacking?

Yes, in the red-team sense. The scientific point is that CKA is conditional on stimuli.

Are dog breeds a true single class?

No. They are a fine-grained superclass with residual breed structure.

Does this mean CKA is useless?

No. It means CKA needs stimulus controls and careful objective design.

What about proxy mismatch?

The Red proxy overestimated hidden score, but the direction transferred.
